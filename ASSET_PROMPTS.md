# Asset prompts for Claude Design

## Zones 2 to 6 (step 9a)

Paste one prompt at a time into Claude Design. Bring each finished render back to Claude Code, which rebuilds it in Studio to match. Every zone uses the same alley layout: 6 lanes side by side, the same lane length, and the bowler at the near end. Only the look changes.

---

### Zone 1: Back Alley (new starting zone, 10 pins)

```
Create a modular set of 3D Roblox-ready props for a run-down city bowling alley, the very first zone of a Roblox bowling simulator for kids and teens. Make each prop a separate, low-polygon model and list its triangle count. Budget: under 3,000 triangles for the lane, under 1,500 for each other prop. Export each as its own .fbx with the pivot at the bottom centre, and use flat colours or one small texture per prop (no real brands, no real tags, no text except "LANES").

Size (Roblox studs, 1 stud is about 28 cm): the lane piece is 94 studs long and 13.4 studs wide including both gutters. The wood is 8 studs wide and each gutter is 0.6 studs deep. The bowler stands at one end and the pins sit 70 studs further down. The game places 6 copies of the lane side by side, so the lane's long edges must line up cleanly with a copy of itself.

Props:
- one cracked, patched single bowling lane with a few missing boards (keep the middle strip, where the ball rolls, flat and unbroken)
- dented trash cans and an overflowing dumpster
- stacked wooden crates and pallets
- a graffiti-covered brick wall piece that tiles side by side (original graffiti, no real tags)
- a neon sign that reads "LANES" with one broken letter (the game makes it flicker)
- a steam vent and a hanging bare light bulb
- a few small scrap props: tires, cardboard boxes, a shopping cart
- a leaning fence piece and a worn tire-and-board bench

Mood: gritty and muted, with small neon accents so it still feels like this game. This is where the player starts, so it should feel like a place worth escaping, but friendly, not scary.

Please also give me:
1. A hero render from behind the bowler looking down one lane, with the props placed around it the way they should sit in the game.
2. A wide render showing all 6 lanes side by side with the props arranged.
3. A colour palette with hex codes: lane wood, gutters, brick, ground, neon accent, light bulb glow, fog colour.
4. One line on the background music feel.
```

## Back Alley extras (zone 1, after 9a part 1)

How long zone 1 lasts: zone 2 opens at rank level 5 = 1,314 XP. A zone 1 throw gives about 15 to 20 XP, so that is about 70 throws, roughly 5 minutes (a bit longer once the slower starting throw from Addendum 3 lands). Short zone, so: 1 crate and 4 balls (2 Common, 1 Uncommon, 1 Rare). The Rare is there because the gift crate at about 4 minutes always gives a Rare, so the zone's crate needs one, and a rare "chase" ball keeps people opening.

Paste one prompt at a time into Claude Design (the same project as the Back Alley props is fine). Bring the renders back to Claude Code.

### Back Alley pins (10 per lane)

```
Design bowling pins for the first zone of a Roblox bowling simulator for kids and teens. The zone is "Back Alley": a run-down outdoor city alley with cracked lanes, brick walls with graffiti, trash cans, a flickering pink "LANES" neon sign, muted foggy colours with small neon accents.

Size (Roblox studs, 1 stud is about 28 cm): each pin is 2.8 studs tall, about 0.95 studs across at its widest. Pivot at the bottom centre, standing upright. Low polygon: under 600 triangles per pin. Keep the outline a classic bowling pin shape so it knocks over and reads clearly from 70 studs away; put the wear in the colours and in small shapes, not in a broken outline.

Make 3 versions with the same shape:
1. Standard pin: old, scuffed off-white with chipped paint, a faded red band at the neck, a strip of grey duct tape around the belly. Look like someone saved them from the trash.
2. Golden pin (a rare bonus pin): the same beat-up pin but in worn brass-gold, with the tape still on.
3. Golden Star pin (even rarer): brighter gold with a small pink neon star on the front that glows.

No text, no real brands. One small texture per pin or flat colours.

Please give me:
1. A render of a full 10-pin triangle standing on a cracked lane at the end of the alley, seen from the bowler (70 studs away).
2. A close-up of each of the 3 pins side by side.
3. Hex codes for every colour (body, band, tape, chips, gold, star glow).
4. The triangle count of each pin.
```

### Back Alley balls (4 balls) and crate (1 crate)

```
Design 4 bowling balls and 1 loot crate for the first zone of a Roblox bowling simulator for kids and teens. The zone is "Back Alley": a run-down outdoor city alley with cracked lanes, graffiti walls, trash cans, a flickering pink "LANES" neon sign, muted foggy colours with small neon accents. These are the first balls a new player collects, made from junk and street stuff, but each should feel like a fun prize.

Ball rules: each ball is 1.8 studs across (Roblox studs). Under 2,500 triangles each. The ball must stay a smooth, round sphere because it rolls: decoration goes in colours, painted shapes and very flat details, nothing sticking out more than 0.05 studs. Pivot at the centre. Keep the 3 finger holes. One small texture per ball or flat colours. No text, no real brands.

The 4 balls (rarity colour in brackets, the game draws a glow in that colour):
1. "Duct Tape" (Common, grey): a dull grey ball wrapped in criss-crossing strips of silver tape.
2. "Rust Bucket" (Common, grey): an old rusty metal ball with bolts and an orange-brown rust pattern.
3. "Hubcap" (Uncommon, green): a shiny chrome ball like a car hubcap, with spokes painted around the finger holes.
4. "Dumpster Diamond" (Rare, blue): a grimy dark ball with cracks, and bright blue crystal glowing through the cracks. This is the rare one everyone wants, so it should be the best looking of the four.

For each ball also suggest a trail colour and one short effect idea for when it rolls (sparks, dust, glints, etc.).

The crate: "Alley Crate". A beat-up wooden crate held together with tape and a bungee cord, a padlock, stencil-style spray paint marks (shapes only, no words), one small pink neon strip. Around 3 studs wide. Under 1,500 triangles. The lid must be a separate piece hinged at the back edge so the game can flip it open. Pivot at the bottom centre.

Please give me:
1. All 4 balls side by side, then a close-up of each from the front (finger holes visible).
2. Hex codes for each ball's colours and suggested trail colour.
3. The crate closed, and the crate with the lid open and light spilling out.
4. The triangle count of each model.
```

### Crate dealer: Rizzo's cart (one stand in the Back Alley)

```
Design a crate dealer character and his stand for the first zone of a Roblox bowling simulator for kids and teens. The zone is "Back Alley": a run-down outdoor city alley with cracked lanes, graffiti walls, trash cans, a flickering pink "LANES" neon sign, muted foggy colours with small neon accents.

The character: Rizzo, a fast-talking, friendly street hustler who sells loot crates from a rusty cart. Fun and a little sneaky, never scary, fine for young kids. Catchphrase: "Luck's a lane, kid." He must be built like a standard Roblox R15 avatar (blocky Roblox body proportions, 5 studs tall) so the game can use Roblox's default idle and wave animations: no extra limbs, no tail, nothing that needs its own animation. Give him clothing colours and simple accessories that can be separate small models: a backwards cap, a too-big puffy jacket covered in patches, fingerless gloves, a bowling-pin keychain, sunglasses pushed up on the cap. Under 4,000 triangles for all accessories together.

The stand: his rusty cart, about 8 studs wide and 5 studs deep. A shopping-cart-meets-street-food-cart: dented metal, mismatched wheels, a striped torn awning, a small pink neon crate-shaped sign (no words), a stack of the zone's crates (beat-up wooden crates with tape and a bungee cord) and one crate on display on a little turntable in front. A spot in front where the player stands to buy. Under 3,000 triangles for the cart. Pivot at the bottom centre. No real brands, no text.

Please give me:
1. A render of Rizzo standing behind his cart in the alley, seen from where the player walks up.
2. A character sheet of Rizzo: front, side and back, with hex codes for skin, every clothing colour and accessories.
3. The cart alone from the front and from a 3/4 angle.
4. The triangle count of each model.
5. 5 short lines Rizzo could say (2 sentences max each, readable by a 9 year old): greeting, when you open a crate, when you get something rare, when you can't afford one, goodbye.
```
